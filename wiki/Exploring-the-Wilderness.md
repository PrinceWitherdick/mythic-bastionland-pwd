# Exploring the Wilderness

Once a Realm is made, the Company travels it hex by hex. The system watches the Company Token and tells the Referee what each move calls for, makes the Wilderness Roll, runs the exploration actions, and keeps the travel rules beside the map with a button for each roll. This page is the Referee's guide to all of it.

## The travel rules beside the map

![The Gravenmoor Realm with the Travel panel against the map's left edge and Rest and Exploration against its right, with the Referee's roll buttons](images/exploring-the-wilderness-rules-panels.webp)

On a Realm Scene, two panels stand against the map's edges, for everyone:

| Panel | Groups |
| --- | --- |
| **Travel** (left) | **On the Road**: Trek, Gallop and Cruise, the Wilderness Roll, Myth Hexes, Omens and Barriers. **Other Travelling Tables**: Travelling Blind, Dire Weather and Local Mood. |
| **Rest and Exploration** (right) | **Rest and Hardship**: Hospitality, Camping, Supplies, Night, Sleep and Winter. **Exploration**: the Lay of the Land, actions, Saves, Searching and Vision. **Gathering Folklore**. |

Once your PDF is imported, the rules are shown in the book's own words; until then a line asks you to import it.

- **Click a group's heading** to fold it. The page number beside a heading opens your rulebook at that page. **Fold the rules away** folds the whole panel to its title; **Show the rules** opens it again. Each browser keeps its own folds and where each panel was scrolled to.
- The panels follow the map as you pan and zoom.
- While a Realm is being drawn, or a map lined up, the panels make way.

For a **Referee**, each section holds the button for what it describes:

| Button | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gallop** | Trek, Gallop and Cruise | Asks who gallops; each steed loses d6 VIG. |
| **Wilderness Roll for the Company** | Wilderness Roll | See [below](#the-wilderness-roll). |
| **Roll Travelling Blind**, **Roll Dire Weather**, **Roll Local Mood** | Other Travelling Tables | Rolls that table. |
| **Lose d6** and a Virtue | Supplies, Night, Sleep, Winter | Asks who suffers that hardship and rolls their losses. |
| **Search the Hex**, **Look from a Vantage Point** | Searching, Vision | See [Exploring a hex](#exploring-a-hex). |
| **Ask Somebody** | Gathering Folklore | See [Exploring a hex](#exploring-a-hex). |

The section that presses now is marked: Night at night, Sleep and Supplies each morning, Winter all Winter. Its **Lose d6** button is filled red.

**Players** get **Ask the Referee** buttons instead, for a Gallop, a Wilderness Roll, a search, a vantage point or folklore. Pressing one whispers the Referees a card naming who asks, with a button that does it.

To hide the panels in your own browser only, untick **Travel Rules beside the Realm Map** in your settings. A Referee who hides them hides their buttons too.

## When the Company moves

One Token stands for the whole Company, and every player can move it. As it comes into a hex, the Referees are whispered a **Where the Company Came** card when the move calls for something:

- **A Myth's hex**: entering it shows the Myth's next Omen. **Reveal its next Omen** does it.
- **A Holding**: **Local Mood** rolls how things stand there.
- **A second hex this Phase** is a Gallop: **Gallop** costs each steed d6 VIG. A third is a Cruise, and further is noted as beyond any way of travelling.
- **Dire weather** keeps the Company in its hex. **Keep them where they were** moves the Token back.

Nothing is spent until you press a button, so a Token dragged by mistake costs nothing.

When the Company comes to rest, Places opens on its new hex for you (see [Places and the Lay of the Land](Places-and-the-Lay-of-the-Land#opening-places)), and a wilderness hex with nothing in it yet has its land rolled.

## Barriers

A Token can't cross a Barrier or leave the edge of the map. The move is refused with a warning, on whoever's screen made it.

- **A hidden Barrier** a player's Token runs into is revealed on the map for everyone, and the Referees are told who found it. This needs a Referee signed in.
- **A Referee's own move** leaves a hidden Barrier hidden, except in [solo play](Solo-Play).
- **A Referee with Foundry's Unconstrained Movement on** passes Barriers, as it passes walls.
- **The Company's attempt wastes the Phase.** The Phase's end window opens with the Company travelling and the Barrier named, offering **Waste the Phase**. Close it to let the Phase stand.

Each Barrier met is noted in the hex the Token was turned back into, and shows on the Journey page of Places.

## The Wilderness Roll

Make it from:

- **Wilderness Roll** in the Realm tools (the crown), for the hex the Company stands in;
- **Wilderness Roll for the Company** on the Travel panel;
- **Wilderness Roll here** in a hex's Lay of the Land, in Places;
- the end of a Phase, in the Phase's end window.

It asks whether the Company ended the Phase **Travelling** or **Camping outdoors**, then rolls and whispers the Referees a card with what came of it: all clear, an Omen from the nearest Myth or a random one, the Myth's own next Omen in its hex, or the hex's Landmark. A Holding isn't Wilderness, so there is no roll there.

- **A Landmark found** is revealed on the map, and named from the prompt a random page of your book prints for its kind if it had no name. A Sanctum names the Seer who lives there.
- **An Omen** is counted on its Myth.
- **Hex chips** on the card name each hex: point at one to ring it on the map, click it to go there.

## Landmarks

The Wilderness card, and the Landmark tab of a hex in Places, carry what each kind of Landmark asks of the Company:

| Landmark | Button | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monument | **Spend a Phase: Restore SPI** | Tick who takes part; their SPI is restored in full, as a Sacrament would. The Phase's end comes next. |
| Hazard | **Push Through** | Asks which Virtue the Hazard takes (most often VIG); everybody who pushes through loses d6 of it. |
| Hazard | **Go Back** | Sends the Company Token back to the hex it came from. |
| Ruin | **Echo a Myth** | Rolls a Myth the Realm doesn't hold and whispers it to the Referees, to hint at. |
| Curse | | Throws the Company off course: travel in the next Phase is travelling blind. |

While a Curse holds, the Referee Toolkit's Time page shows **Thrown Off Course**, with **Roll Travelling Blind** and **They Hold Their Course** to let the blight go. During the cursed Phase, each move of the Company Token is rolled for already.

An **Other** Landmark is one of your own. It has no button: give it a name in Places, and keep what it holds in the hex's note.

### More Landmarks from the blog

Chris McDowall, who made Mythic Bastionland, offers six more Landmarks in a post on his blog, More Landmarks. They aren't from the book, so they're off until the Referee turns on **More Landmarks (Blog)** in the world settings (see [World Settings Reference](World-Settings-Reference)). While it's on, they are rolled into new Realms and offered wherever you place a Landmark. Turning it off again leaves those already placed where they are.

Each one's tab in Places says, in the system's own words, what it is and links the post. Some carry more:

| Landmark | In Places | Found by the Wilderness Roll |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bridge | **Passage won**: tick it once a Knight has beaten the Bridge's keeper. You're asked which Knights won it, and each gains 1 Glory. A Bridge gives its Glory only once. | As usual. |
| Pond | **Leads To**: a hex chip for the Pond it's paired with. Point at it to ring that hex; click it to go there. A Pond with no partner on this Realm leads beyond it. | Only on a 6. |
| Chapel | | As usual. |
| Grotto | Which of its three it is. | Never: only a local's directions lead to it, so reveal it yourself in **Edit this hex**. |
| Fountain | Which of its three it is. | As usual. |
| Willow | Which of its three it is, and whether it stands this Season. | Only in its own Season. |

A rolled Realm has two Bridges, a pair of Ponds, two Chapels, and three each of Grottos, Fountains and Willows, one of each sort, with fewer on a smaller map. Bridges are laid on a river where there is one.

## Exploring a hex

Three actions, each from the Rest and Exploration panel or from a hex's Lay of the Land. Their cards are whispered to the Referees.

### Ask Somebody

Asks **Who is asked**: **A Vassal**, **A Knight or Vagabond**, or **A Seer**. Each knows a different amount, read off the map: the nearest Myth and which way it lies, a random Myth and the nearest Landmark, or everything. **Ask Them** posts a **Gathering Folklore** card listing the Myths and Landmarks they know, each with a hex chip where they can name the hex.

### Search the Hex

Spends a Phase searching. Choose what it is spent on:

- **Sweep the whole Hex**: a quick look over it.
- **Search for something known to be here**: a Save. Choose who rolls and with which Virtue, type the d20 if it was rolled at the table or leave it empty to roll here, and say what they seek. A failure says the goal may still be had at a cost.
- **Climb somewhere high**: a vantage point.

**The Phase passes** is ticked by default; untick it where you rule otherwise.

### Look from a Vantage Point

The Company takes in this hex and a rough idea of the land around: the neighbours' terrain and the Barriers between. Myths and Landmarks out there keep their secrets.

Where something stands in a hex around, the **Seen from Afar** window lists it. Tick each one to mark, add a few words of what they see if you like, and press **Mark Their Map**. Each gets an unnamed mark on the players' map, and the readout says **Something stands here**, until the Company gets there.

### Mark it on the players' map

Folklore and survey cards carry one **Mark it on the players' map** button. It reveals what the card names that the players could place: the Myths their teller could point to, the Landmarks, and Barriers seen from a vantage point.

## Weather

With [FXMaster](Installing-and-Recommended-Modules) on, the weather falls on the Scene the players are on.

- Each morning, the Phase's end window has a **Roll the day's sky and weather** tick under **Breaking Camp**. The card it posts says **The map now shows** whatever sky was rolled.
- The weather button on the Referee Toolkit's banner opens a picker: Clear, Fair, Overcast, Windy, Fog, Rain, Downpour, Storm, Snow and Blizzard. **Take It Off the Map** keeps the weather set but stops it falling.
- Fog can hide the way. The card offers **Let the Fog Hide the Way** and **The Fog Lifts**; while it's down, a Company without a way to keep its course travels blind.
- Each part of the weather (clouds, fog, rain, hail, snow, blizzard snow, storm light) can be turned off under **Weather on the Map** on the Toolkit's Settings page.

See [Time, Phases and Seasons](Time-Phases-and-Seasons) for the Phase's end window.

## Reading the map

![The hex readout under the map, reading the Company's hex as (5, 9) Forest, visited, with the Company's note on it](images/exploring-the-wilderness-readout.webp)

### The hex readout

Point at a hex of a Realm and a label under the map's bottom edge names it: its column and row, its terrain, what stands there, any Barrier, whether the Company has been there, what the players were told, and the Company's note. It shows only while the pointer is over a hex. A player sees only what they have found, and a hex's name only if it is in their Places. A Referee sees everything, with what's hidden marked **(hidden)**.

- **Name the Realm Hex under the Pointer** hides the label, in your browser only.
- **Show Hex Column and Row** (a world setting, on by default) names the column and row first. Untick it to leave them off for everyone.

### Hex chips on chat cards

Every card that names a hex carries a chip for it: the Wilderness card, the Realm Key, folklore and survey cards, the hex rolls, people and more.

- **Point at a chip** to ring that hex on the map.
- **Click it** to go there, switching to that Realm's Scene first if another is up. Only your own view moves.

### The green ring

**Ping** in the Places title bar, and the crosshair on a Places row, show a hex on the map ringed in green. The ring stays until your next click on the map.
